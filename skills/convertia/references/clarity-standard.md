# Silent clarity standard

The user rejected a report that was structured but still dense. Headings, emoji and a table do not make difficult prose clear. Rewrite the sentences themselves. Apply this to all modes and every section.

## Draft, edit, preserve, deliver

- Draft: decide the answer, evidence, condition and action before choosing layout.
- Edit: replace abstract phrasing with the person, action and object. Split a sentence when its clauses answer different questions. Explain a term at its first useful appearance.
- Preserve: compare rewritten claims with their sources. Keep quantities, units, negation, uncertainty, exceptions and the scope of each claim intact.
- Deliver: put the decision and important warning in the first view. Keep supporting explanation in short bullets. Put all sources at the absolute bottom.

Aim for a 350-600-word report with 2-4 bullets per ordinary section. Expand only for requested depth or a material condition. Follow-ups should usually contain an answer and an example, with a warning only when it changes the decision. Do not pad them.

## Corrections from the rejected output

| Dense wording | Clear replacement |
|---|---|
| Sell better allocation of feasibility spending, supported by traceable findings. | Help owners decide which buildings deserve a full study. Show the evidence behind that recommendation. |
| Establish willingness to pay from a budget holder. | Ask the person who controls the budget to approve a paid pilot. |
| An attractive property conversion does not prove a profitable screening service. | A building conversion can make money while Convertia loses money on the screening. Calculate both separately. |
| Positive contribution after counting founder time and review costs. | The fee must cover your delivery time, expert review and other direct costs. Sales costs and overhead still need to be covered. |
| Record a second-asset request separately from the first purchase. | Track whether the same customer brings a second building. One purchase does not prove repeat demand. |
| No unresolved missed blocker in the tested cases. | Have a qualified reviewer check for serious problems the screening missed. Fix those gaps before taking another case. |
| Revisit the segment or trigger. | If owners will not share a real case, check whether you chose the wrong buyers or a problem they do not need to solve now. |

## Meaning-preservation traps

| Bad simplification | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| Conversion is allowed. | The plan may allow the proposed use. Permit and technical checks remain separate. |
| The screening earns SEK 4000. | In this hypothetical example, SEK 4000 remains after direct costs, before sales costs and overhead. |
| Customers need this. | One practitioner described the problem. Buyer demand is still untested. |
| Three tests prove accuracy. | Three cases can reveal errors. They cannot establish general accuracy. |
| No permit is needed. | Permit requirements are unverified for this property; check current rules and the proposed change. |

## Private release checklist

Read every section, bullet, source label and action once more. Can a reader say what it means without translating business jargon? Does each block answer one question? Are the decisive condition and next step visible? Are repeated conclusions removed? Are terms defined before they are used? Are examples clearly hypothetical? Does the follow-up answer only its question? Revise failures before sending.

Do not display this checklist or a clarity score. Do not claim a statistical score or tool execution. The supplied Clarity skill's measurement methods inform these editing checks; no metric threshold proves comprehension, and no script can certify truth or legal meaning.
