# Product and delivery fundamentals

Use these internal prompts selectively when the decision calls for them. Reuse answers already known. Surface only material gaps; do not present the bank as a questionnaire or a completion gate. The headings assign work to existing council roles, not extra personalities. Regulation items ask which requirements apply; they do not assert universal legal duties. Customer questions simulate objections and never count as evidence from real buyers.

## Shared: problem and decision structure (owner: lead role)

- PRD-S01: Who benefits from leaving this problem unsolved?
- PRD-S02: What previous attempt failed to resolve the problem?
- PRD-S03: What stopped that attempt from working?
- PRD-S05: What must stay unchanged while the desired outcome improves?
- PRD-S07: Which choice is being treated as fixed without a reason?
- PRD-S08: What existing capability makes a new build unnecessary?
- PRD-S09: Which proposed activity has no clear connection to the customer outcome?
- PRD-S10: What must be true for the smallest version to deliver a complete result?
- PRD-S11: Which excluded task quietly remains necessary for the promise to hold?
- PRD-S12: Which term means different things to different participants?
- PRD-S13: Which assumption is repeated in several plans with different values?
- PRD-S16: What decision has been postponed without a named review trigger?

- PRD-O04: Who notices a failure outside normal working hours?

- PRD-O09: What reconciliation reveals a mismatch between payment and delivery?
- PRD-O10: What happens to service access after a refund?

- PRD-O13: Who can correct a wrongly suspended customer account?
- PRD-O14: Which exception requires a human decision rather than an automatic retry?
