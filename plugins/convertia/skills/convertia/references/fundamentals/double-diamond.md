# Double Diamond: problem and solution exploration

Use when a team is framing a problem, choosing an approach or testing delivery. Discover and Develop broaden the options; Define and Deliver narrow them. Return to an earlier phase when new evidence changes the problem. These are prompts for judgment, not a required linear process. A small prototype can help discovery.

## Discover: understand the situation
- DD-001: Whose experience is absent from our understanding of this problem?
- DD-002: What did we observe in the customer's actual setting?
- DD-003: Which complaint describes a symptom rather than its cause?
- DD-004: What workaround reveals a need people have not stated?
- DD-005: Whose experience contradicts the dominant account?
- DD-006: What constraint have we assumed without checking?

## Define: choose the problem
- DD-007: What problem can we state without naming our preferred solution?
- DD-008: What changed in our problem definition after the research?
- DD-009: Which need takes priority when stakeholder needs conflict?
- DD-010: What boundary makes this challenge practical to investigate?
- DD-011: What observable outcome would count as an improvement?
- DD-012: What evidence would make us reopen the problem definition?

## Develop: compare possible responses
- DD-013: What substantially different response could address the same need?
- DD-014: What could solve the problem without a new product?
- DD-015: Who affected by the decision helped shape these options?
- DD-016: Which assumption does each prototype isolate?
- DD-017: What common criteria make the alternatives comparable?
- DD-018: Which rejected option deserves another look after new evidence?

## Deliver: test and improve in use
- DD-019: What must the smallest real-world trial teach us?
- DD-020: What feedback can reveal harm during the trial?
- DD-021: What finding would cause us to revise the chosen solution?
- DD-022: What finding would send us back to the problem itself?
- DD-023: Who will monitor the outcome after handover?
- DD-024: What can a limited trial not establish about wider delivery?

These original questions apply the framework to venture advice. They do not certify design quality or require four sections in the user's answer.
